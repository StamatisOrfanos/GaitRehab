\begin{abstract}
Early identification of gait cycle asymmetries plays a critical role in effective stroke rehabilitation and recovery. This study presents 
a novel approach to classify gait cycle symmetry levels using gyroscope signals from shank-mounted inertial measurement units (IMUs). 
Building upon a previously collected dataset, we segmented the walking data into individual gait cycles and annotated each with a three-tier 
confidence level indicating the degree of asymmetry high, moderate, and low. The confidence levels were mapped to a binary classification 
task of symmetrical against asymmetrical gait cycles. 



Our preliminary testing on healthy individuals demonstrates the feasibility of real-time symmetry analysis using minimal sensor input. 
This work lays the groundwork for future clinical deployment in post-stroke rehabilitation settings.
\end{abstract}
