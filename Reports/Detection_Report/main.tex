\begin{abstract}
Early identification of gait cycle asymmetries plays a critical role in effective stroke rehabilitation and recovery. This study presents 
a novel approach to classify gait cycle symmetry levels using gyroscope signals from shank-mounted inertial measurement units. 
Building upon a previously collected dataset, we segmented the walking data into individual gait cycles, annotated each with a three-tier 
confidence level (high, moderate, low) based on the symmetrical difference of the movements of each leg. Features were extracted from the
gait cycles, including stance and stride durations for each leg, and machine learning models were trained to classify the gait cycles as symmetric or asymmetric. 
The models achieved an accuracy of 90\% and an f1-score of 94\% using ensemble methods like Random Forest and Gradient Boosting on the moderate confidence 
level, with even better results for the high confidence level. Preliminary testing on healthy individuals using a smartphone connected to the gyroscope sensors 
demonstrates the feasibility of real-time symmetry analysis with minimal sensor input. This approach has the potential to enhance stroke rehabilitation by 
providing real-time feedback on gait symmetry, enabling timely interventions and personalized rehabilitation strategies.

\end{abstract}
