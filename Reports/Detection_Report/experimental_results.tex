\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:experimental_resuts}

\subsection{Model Accuracy Metrics}
The classification performance across the three gait asymmetry confidence levels—high, moderate, and low, demonstrates consistent trends in model behavior 
and discriminative capacity as seen on the Table \ref{tab:confidence_metrics_clean}. At the high-confidence level, the best overall performance was achieved by 
K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), which yielded accuracy of $0.93$ and a balanced F1-score of $0.95$, closely followed by XGBoost and Random Forest, both of which also 
achieved AUC scores above $0.94$. Notably, all support vector machine (SVM) variants reached perfect precision ($1.0$), but at the cost of lower recall, indicating 
conservative but specific predictions.

At the moderate-confidence level, XGBoost was the top performer with an F1-score of $0.94$ and AUC of $0.95$, closely followed by KNN and Random 
Forest, all of which maintained a strong balance between sensitivity and specificity. Logistic Regression, while achieving high precision ($0.97$), showed 
comparatively lower recall ($0.82$), reinforcing its tendency toward under-detection of asymmetric gait patterns in this setting.

At the low-confidence level, the KNN classifier outperformed other models with the highest accuracy ($0.92$), F1-score ($0.95$), and AUC ($0.95$). 
XGBoost and Random Forest followed closely, demonstrating strong generalization even under noisy or subtly asymmetric conditions.

\subsection{Confusion Matrices and Model Behavior}
To further interpret model behavior, we examined the confusion matrices for each confidence level. At the high-confidence level, the best-performing model (KNN)
achieved a near-perfect balance, correctly identifying the majority of stroke-related gait cycles with only a small number of false positives and false negatives 
(e.g., 22 FN, 30 FP). This indicates both high sensitivity and specificity in cases of clear asymmetry. For the moderate-confidence group, a slight increase in 
false negatives was observed, reflecting the ambiguous nature of borderline gait cycles. In the low-confidence group, while overall accuracy remained high, the 
confusion matrices showed a relative increase in misclassifications—particularly false negatives—suggesting the limits of distinguishability in near-symmetric 
gait windows. These patterns support the model's robustness in confidently asymmetric cycles and its graceful degradation in lower-confidence scenarios.
