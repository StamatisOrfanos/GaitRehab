\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
A gait cycle is the complete sequence of movements by one limb from initial contact to the next initial contact on the same side—typically divided into stance 
(60\% of the cycle) and swing phases (40\% of the cycle) and further subdivided into clinically relevant events such as heel-strike and toe-off \cite{ref1,ref2}.

Clinical gait cycle analysis—traditionally done in motion laboratories using tools like optical motion capture, force plates, and electromyography—provides precise 
temporal, spatial and kinematic data essential for diagnosing abnormalities and planning interventions \cite{ref3,ref4}. However, the high cost, spatial requirements,
and complexity of these systems limit their use for frequent assessments, especially in stroke rehabilitation where gait training must be performed 
regularly \cite{ref5}.

Wearable technologies, particularly Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) and pressure sensors, offer portable alternatives to lab-based gait cycle analysis. These 
devices enable the extraction of interpretable gait features—even in home settings—and have been successfully combined with machine learning methods to detect 
and quantify gait cycle events and pathological deviations \cite{ref6,ref7}. While advanced classifier frameworks (e.g., SVMs, CNNs, LSTMs) achieve high detection 
accuracies, they often rely on extensive feature extraction pipelines and substantial computational resources, limiting their deployment in real-time or 
resource-constrained environments \cite{ref8}.

In this study, we proposed a lightweight pipeline to analyze gait cycle asymmetries using bilateral gyroscope data. The method focuses on extrema-based statistical 
features and traditional machine learning models, aiming for interpretability, efficiency, and suitability for embedded or web-based rehabilitation platforms. 

The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A gait cycle labeling method that segments walking data into individual gait cycles and annotates them with a three-tier confidence levels based on the 
    symmetrical difference of each leg's movements.
    \item A minimal feature extraction method using bilateral gyroscope signals for gait cycle asymmetry detection.
    \item A comparative evaluation of traditional machine learning models.
    \item Demonstration of high classification performance across all accuracy metrics with reduced computational complexity.
    \item Preliminary validation on healthy subjects, showcasing the feasibility of real-time gait symmetry analysis.
\end{itemize}
