\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The experimental results demonstrate that even with a minimal set of biomechanically meaningful features like the minimum and maximum 
values of z-axis gyroscope angular velocity from both legs—machine, we developed lightweight machine learning models that achieved highly accurate 
gait classification. Notably, the best-performing models (KNN, SVM with Linear and RBF kernels, and Logistic Regression) consistently achieved 
accuracy scores of 94.44\% and an AUC of 1.00 under Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV), underscoring the strong discriminatory power of these features.

An interesting observation is the similar performance among all SVM variants, which supports the hypothesis that gait patterns exhibit linearly 
and non-linearly separable structures. On the other hand, XGBoost performed relatively poorly with an AUC of 0.60, suggesting that tree-based methods 
may be less effective with such a low-dimensional, dense feature set. This performance discrepancy highlights the importance of model selection in 
small-feature, high-signal contexts.The confusion matrices support the robustness of the top models, showing very low rates of misclassification, especially 
for models like KNN and Logistic Regression, which only misclassified a single instance.

These findings also emphasize the practical benefit of interpretable and simple features. By focusing on maximum and minimum angular 
velocities metrics that can be understood by clinicians and possibly influenced by patient training, this approach supports not only 
automated classification but also explainable feedback loops in rehabilitation settings.
